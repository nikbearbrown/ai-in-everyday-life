# Run of show

## Executive summary

**What this is.** How the workshops fit together into one session: formats, roles, group formation and the AI-use policy.

**Why read it.** Session length varies, so the plan is built from **modules** that snap into a 90-minute, 2-hour or half-day program. Pick the column that fits your slot.

**What it decides.**

1. The workshops are designed to be taught by **graduate students**.
2. The day has three parts (master class, innovation lab, graduate opportunities), with a pitch round as the payoff.
3. **The world has changed, so the day teaches two things together:** master the tools, and keep what is irreducibly human. Every module has both halves. See [THE-THROUGH-LINE.md](THE-THROUGH-LINE.md).
4. Students drive the AI; coaches coach. Every group fills in a Driver's Ledger and pitches both hands.
5. Everything works with no code and no student accounts, with an unplugged fallback for every module.

## Run of show, three formats

| Block | 90 min | 120 min (default) | 180 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome and icebreaker (00) | 5, welcome only | 10 | 10 |
| Master class (01, 02, 03) | 20 (01 and 02) | 30 (all three) | 35 (fuller) |
| Canvas and group formation (09) | 5 | 5 | 15, with ethics cards |
| Innovation lab (04 to 08) | 30 | 40 | 60, or 40 plus Bonus 12 |
| Pitches (10) | 20 | 25 | 35, with judging and recognitions |
| Close (11) | 10 | 10 | 25, with graduate-student Q&A |
| **Total** | **90** | **120** | **180** |

At 90 minutes, run the canvas as a half page and trim the lab to the first four steps. At 180 minutes, add a break, the bonus for a more technical table, and a graduate-student panel.

## The AI-use policy

**Default.** One shared laptop per table (the coach's), one general-purpose AI assistant, no student accounts, no personal data in any prompt. **A student drives the keyboard; the coach never does. The driver rotates every four to five minutes.** This resolves Wi-Fi, accounts and privacy at once, and keeps students' hands on the tool, which is the "master it" half.

Optional: students may use their own devices on free accounts if the host organization is comfortable with it. See [FACILITATOR-GUIDE.md](FACILITATOR-GUIDE.md).

## Who does what (about 7 graduate students)

| Role | Count | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Master class lead** | 1 | Runs 01, 02, 03, and builds the Error Bank in module 02 |
| **Table coaches** | 5 | One per lab track. Each owns a guide (04 to 08), coaches one group, helps them pitch |
| **Emcee and timekeeper** | 1 (may be the lead) | Keeps the clock, runs 00 and 10 |
| **Documenter** | 1 | Photos and recording for the films, **only with confirmed consent** |
| **Faculty or staff anchor** | 1 | Runs the close (11), answers admissions questions |

Recruit 8 or 9 to land 6 or 7. One coach, one group, the whole lab. A student who sees the same graduate student from kickoff to pitch gets a mentor.

## Group formation

20 students, mixed on purpose, groups of four with one or two coders each. Say it out loud: *the coder is not the leader; the person who understands the user is.* Rotate who drives the keyboard.
