# 02. Catch the AI Being Wrong

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Master class, whole room.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A 10-minute game in which students judge five real AI answers **before** checking them, then find out which were wrong. **Why run it.** The most valuable human skill in the room is the one that cannot be handed over: smelling a wrong answer. AI output is fluent, and fluency makes us trust it more, not less. The game trains the habit *verdict first, lookup second*. **What students leave with.** A three-part habit they use in every lab: **verdict, target, mechanism.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 10 minutes |
| **Group** | Whole room, in groups of four |
| **Materials** | Five printed "answer cards," a worksheet, one laptop for the reveal |
| **Tech** | Light. A search or a calculator for the reveal. |
| **Lead** | Master class lead |

## The two jobs in this module

- **Master it:** Check it. Use a check that fails *differently* than the AI: a source, a calculation, a person. Never ask the same AI to grade itself.
- **Keep it human:** The judgment of *could this even be true?* comes before any lookup, and it is yours.

## Prep: the Error Bank (before the day)

The lead builds five answer cards from **real** AI chats, run in advance. Each card shows the question and the AI's answer, and the lead has verified the truth from an independent source and saved it. **Mix them: two wrong, two right, one half-right.** Good sources of subtle wrongness: a made-up citation, a confident number with no source, a plausible rule applied to the wrong situation, a unit mix-up, a summary that adds a claim the original did not make. Do not invent errors. Use real ones the lead actually found. Keep a private answer key with how it was checked.

## Run of show

1. **Rules (1 min).** "For each card: **verdict** (right, wrong, or can't tell), **target** (the exact sentence or number you doubt), **mechanism** (how it could be wrong). You must write all three *before* anyone looks anything up. A correct verdict with no mechanism earns no points."
2. **Prediction-lock (4 min).** Groups work through the five cards on the worksheet. At the end, each group **locks** its answers by signing the bottom. No edits after the reveal.
3. **Reveal (4 min).** The lead shows how each was checked, not just the result. Run each check live where possible. Score the groups. Name the two things that make AI errors hard to spot: they sound confident, and they are usually *almost* right.
4. **The lesson (1 min).** "You did not need to look anything up to be suspicious. That suspicion is a skill. A tool can look things up for you. Only you can know when to bother."

## Say this out loud

"Fluent is not the same as true. The smoother the answer sounds, the more careful you need to be. Today you'll practice being careful on purpose."

## Facilitator notes

- Match scrutiny to use: a typo fix needs a glance; a medical or legal claim needs a source. Ask each group: "At what level were you using it?"
- Never say "AI always lies" or "AI cannot be trusted." Say what the cards showed.
- Praise the *mechanism* more than the verdict. That is the skill.

## Unplugged fallback

The cards are on paper. The reveal can be read from the answer key with the source named. No Wi-Fi needed.

## Watch-outs

- Do not use a card you have not verified yourself. If you are unsure of the truth, drop it.
- Do not shame a wrong answer. Everyone gets some wrong. That is the point.
