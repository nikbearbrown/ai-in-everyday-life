# 04. Build: A 3D Model (the Delivery Ship)

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. One group of about five, 65 minutes.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A group studies twelve photos of a teal cartoon delivery spaceship, measures its proportions with a ruler, has an AI build a 3D model in the browser that they can spin on screen, and compares it to the photos to find where the AI made things up. **Why this track.** It is the most physical and the most fun. It starts with a pencil and ends with an object. And it shows the central skill plainly: **you can see that the model is wrong because you looked at the photos.** **What the group leaves with.** A rotatable 3D ship, a measurement sheet, and a list of every part and proportion the AI invented.

## The two jobs in this track

- **Master it:** Spec it. Turn a drawing into numbers, then the numbers into a prompt the AI can use.
- **Keep it human:** Compare the output to the world, and say what the model is not.

## Prep (coach, before the day)

Print the photos. Confirm where they came from. Run the starter prompt once with your tool, and record how long it took and what it got wrong. Check that the 3D page opens in the browser, and decide how to run it offline (see the kit). Bring a finished example. **The ship is a copyrighted character design and the photos are not in the repository. Keep them classroom-only.**

## Run of show (65 minutes)

1. **Look (5 min).** Sort the photos into front, side, top, back and three-quarter views. List the parts: body, stripe, portholes, dome, tail fin, side fins, engine. Which are simple shapes? Which are hard?
2. **Measure (10 min).** On the side view, call the body length **1.0**. Measure the other parts against it. Do the same on the top view for width. Write the numbers on the print and agree on them as a group. Remember that parts closer to the camera look bigger.
3. **Spec it (5 min).** Three lists: **must match** (the proportions you measured, the stripe, the porthole count), **may decide** (colors, glow), **must ask me** (anything not visible in the photos).
4. **Build (25 min).** The driver sends the photos and the numbers with the starter prompt. Open the result. Rotate it. Rotate the driver every eight minutes. Fix one proportion at a time.
5. **Catch (10 min).** Hold the screen next to the photos. Find where the model is wrong. Ask the AI to list every proportion it invented. Compare that list to what you found. Write the Catch Card.
6. **Ledger and demo prep (10 min).** Fill the ledger, including one line: **what this model is not** (a toy built from simple shapes, not a faithful model).

## The things to catch

The wrong number of portholes. A tail fin that is the wrong height. A missing engine mesh. A logo that is backwards on one side of the fin. A list of "invented parts" that is shorter than the real list.

## Debrief questions

- Which of your measurements did the AI follow? Which did it ignore?
- If someone saw your model without the photos, would they know what it is?

## Unplugged fallback

If the AI chat or 3D page fails, build a cardboard-and-paper model from the measurements and describe the AI's job aloud. If a photo is too small, measure a printed enlargement.

## Watch-outs

- Keep the photos and images of the model off public channels.
- A model that looks right from one angle may be wrong from another. Rotate it.
