# 04. Lab: Campus Concierge

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Small group, 40 minutes. Topic: the student experience at a university.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A group of four designs an AI-assisted way to fix one real friction in student life (finding help, registering, getting advice, feeling lost in the first weeks). **Why run it.** The human skill this lab trains is the first and hardest one: **notice the right problem.** The tool skill is **Spec it.** AI will happily solve whatever problem you hand it, including the wrong one. **What the group leaves with.** A problem framed two ways and tested, a spec written as must/may/ask, an AI-built prototype conversation, a Catch Card, and a Driver's Ledger.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 40 minutes |
| **Group** | 4 students plus one coach |
| **Materials** | The Driver's Canvas, sticky notes, one shared laptop with a general-purpose AI, timer |
| **Tech** | Wi-Fi for AI. Paper fallback below. |
| **Coach owns** | The group for the whole lab, and the pitch |

## The two jobs in this lab

- **Master it:** Spec it. Write what the AI must match, may decide, and must stop and ask about.
- **Keep it human:** Notice the right problem. Two framings of the problem are only different if they would need *different fixes* to test.

## Run of show

1. **Meet the problem (7 min). User hat.** Each student names one real friction from their own campus life. The group picks one. Write **two different framings** of it. Example: "Students can't find the right office" versus "Students don't know which question to ask." **Distinctness test:** list three possible fixes and mark which framing each would solve. If no fix disagrees between the two framings, they are one framing reworded. Rewrite until they differ.
2. **Observed or generated? (3 min). Skeptic hat.** Ask the AI "what is the biggest problem students face?" Tag every sentence **generated** (the AI said so) or **observed** (one of us saw it). For two generated claims, name how you could turn them into observed by watching one person do one task.
3. **Spec it (8 min). Data and Process hats.** On paper, three lists for the helper you want: **Must match** (tone, accuracy, facts it may never invent), **May decide** (wording, order, examples), **Must stop and ask** (anything about a real student, money, mental health, discipline). Name what data it would use and where that data lives.
4. **Direct it (12 min).** One student drives the keyboard; the others direct. Rotate every four minutes. Build: a persona for the helper, five sample conversations (include one confusing question and one "I don't know" case), and a one-page "how it fits the campus" map. Anything the AI invents about the real campus goes in a **verify** column.
5. **Catch Card (5 min). Skeptic hat.** Find one thing the AI got wrong, made up, or assumed. Write it on a Catch Card: *what it said, what was true, how we checked.* If it got everything right, check harder.
6. **Driver's Ledger and pitch outline (5 min).** Fill the ledger. Outline the three-minute pitch: the problem as observed, the artifact, the catch, the ledger.

## Suggested prompts for the coach to hold in reserve

Not to read out. Only if a group is stuck: "Who is the student in this story, in one sentence?" "What would change if the first framing were true?" "What does it need to know that it does not?"

## Debrief questions

- Which framing changed your fix?
- What did you decide that the AI could not?
- What did you not check?

## Unplugged fallback

The coach plays the AI behind a folded paper screen. Students write prompts on slips; the coach answers in a plain, confident voice and, once, includes a plausible mistake. Run the Catch Card as written.

## Watch-outs

- Do not let the group ship the first framing. The distinctness test is the lesson.
- No real student names, IDs or stories about real people. Make the persona up.
- The AI will say it "knows" the campus. It does not. Everything campus-specific is unverified.
