# 08. Lab: Everyday Fixer

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Small group, 40 minutes. Topic: everyday challenges.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A group picks one ordinary, annoying problem from daily life (a messy budget, a roommate chore schedule, planning a group trip, a club that can't keep track of events) and runs the **whole loop**: spec it, direct the AI to build a solution, check it, and sign it. **Why run it.** This is the cleanest practice of all four tool skills, in a setting where everyone is the expert. It is the lab for the student who says "I'm not technical." The human skill is **matching scrutiny to use**: some outputs need a glance and some need a signature. **What the group leaves with.** A working plan or tool they built with AI, a three-level classification of their own AI habits, and a Driver's Ledger.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 40 minutes |
| **Group** | 4 students plus one coach |
| **Materials** | Driver's Canvas, a "levels" card (below), one shared laptop with a general-purpose AI, paper |
| **Tech** | Wi-Fi for AI. Paper fallback below. |

## The two jobs in this lab

- **Master it:** The full loop: spec, direct, check, and chain (a repeatable procedure the group can reuse).
- **Keep it human:** Own the consequence. Decide how closely each output must be checked, and put a name on it.

## The levels card

| Level | You use AI as | How closely you check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A copy editor (fix wording) | A glance |
| 2 | A research assistant (find, summarize) | Check against sources |
| 3 | A collaborator on how you frame the problem | Supervise hard; you own the framing |

## Run of show

1. **Pick the problem (5 min). User hat.** The group picks one. It must be real to at least one of them, small enough to solve in 40 minutes, and have a *person who would be affected* by a wrong answer.
2. **Spec it (7 min).** Must match / may decide / must ask me. Name the data the plan will need and what is missing.
3. **Direct it (14 min).** A student drives; rotate every five minutes. Run the loop: frame, plan, draft, audit, revise. After the first draft, the group must change at least one thing the AI got wrong or assumed. Save both versions.
4. **Classify your habits (5 min).** Each student names one real way they used AI this week. Put it on the levels card. Name the gap between *how you used it* and *how closely you checked it.* A Level 3 use with a Level 1 check is the gap that bites.
5. **Check it and write the Catch Card (4 min). Skeptic hat.** What was the weakest assumption? What would a wrong answer look like here, and who would notice first?
6. **Driver's Ledger and pitch outline (5 min).** Also write a **skill**: three lines the group could paste next time to get the same result (**use when / steps / stop and ask**). This is the Chain-it move in its smallest form.

## Debrief questions

- Which step did the AI do best? Which did it fake?
- Where did you spend your attention?
- What would you not let it do again?

## Unplugged fallback

The coach plays the AI. Groups write the spec on slips of paper and get a plan back; the coach includes one plausible mistake. The Catch Card and ledger run as written.

## Watch-outs

- Keep the problem small. Big problems become brainstorm and nothing is built.
- No real personal data (bank balances, real friend names). Use made-up numbers.
- Do not let "it saved time" count as a ledger entry. Say what was checked.
