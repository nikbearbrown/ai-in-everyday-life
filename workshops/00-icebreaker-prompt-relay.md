# 00. Prompt Relay

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A 10-minute, no-devices icebreaker in which students pass instructions down a line and watch a simple task go wrong. **Why run it.** It teaches the first tool skill, **Spec it**: a precise instruction beats a clever one. It also surfaces the first human truth of the day: *you* were the one who knew what you meant, and nobody else could read your mind. **What students leave with.** A felt sense of why "make me a poster" gets a bad poster and "a poster for X, for Y people, with these three things" gets a good one.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 10 minutes |
| **Group** | Whole room, in two teams of about 10 |
| **Materials** | Paper, pens, one printed picture per team (kept hidden), a timer |
| **Tech** | None |
| **Lead** | Emcee |

## The two jobs in this module

- **Master it:** Spec it. Say what you want, what you do not, and who it is for.
- **Keep it human:** You hold the intent. A spec is how you move what is in your head into someone else's hands.

## Run of show

1. **Set up (1 min).** Each team lines up. The first person sees the picture for 15 seconds. It is a simple scene: for example, a lopsided house with a red door, one tree, a sun with a face.
2. **Round 1: vague (3 min).** Person 1 whispers a one-sentence description to person 2, who whispers it to person 3, and so on. The last person draws it from the sentence alone. Reveal the drawing next to the original.
3. **Round 2: spec (3 min).** New picture. This time person 1 may say three things: **must have**, **may decide**, **ask me if unsure**. The last person draws, and may ask one question back up the line.
4. **Debrief (3 min).**
   - What was lost in round 1? (Everything the first person assumed was obvious.)
   - What did "ask me if unsure" change?
   - **Who here is the AI?** The last person in the line is following instructions with no access to your head. That is what you are doing every time you type a prompt.

## Say this out loud

"The AI is the last person in the line. It is fast, it is willing, and it only has your words. Today you will learn to write better words, and to notice when the drawing is wrong even though it looks great."

## Unplugged and low risk

This module needs nothing. If the room is small, run one team and use two pictures.

## Watch-outs

- Do not let a student feel they "failed." Round 1 is *designed* to go wrong.
- Keep the pictures harmless. No faces of real people.
- If the room is quiet, pick the first speaker yourself.
