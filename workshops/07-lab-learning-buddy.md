# 07. Lab: Learning Buddy

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Small group, 40 minutes. Topic: AI in education.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A group tests two ways of using AI to study the same short topic, compares what each person can actually do afterward, and designs a study buddy that coaches instead of answering. **Why run it.** Every student in the room uses AI for school. The honest question is not "is it cheating?" but "what did I learn?" The human point of this lab is that **the struggle is the point**: when AI does the thinking, you lose the thing you came to build. The tool skill is **Direct it**: make the AI ask you questions instead of handing you answers. **What the group leaves with.** A comparison they ran themselves, a tutor prompt, and a Driver's Ledger.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 40 minutes |
| **Group** | 4 students plus one coach |
| **Materials** | Driver's Canvas, a one-page mini-reading on a neutral topic (see below), one shared laptop with a general-purpose AI, paper |
| **Tech** | Wi-Fi for AI. Paper fallback below. |

## The two jobs in this lab

- **Master it:** Direct it. Make the AI behave as a coach: ask questions, wait, and only hint.
- **Keep it human:** Keep the struggle. Decide which parts of learning you must do yourself, and why a person (a teacher, a classmate) still matters.

## Prep

The coach picks a one-page reading on a neutral topic that nobody in the group already knows well (a short article on how a bridge distributes load, how a vaccine trial is structured, or how a credit score is calculated). Print four copies. Prepare four short questions that test understanding, not recall.

## Run of show

1. **Two ways (12 min). Split the group in two pairs.**
   - **Pair A: "Answer machine."** Ask the AI to explain the topic and summarize the reading. Read the AI's answer. Done.
   - **Pair B: "Coach."** Ask the AI to quiz you on the reading one question at a time, give hints not answers, and not move on until you can explain it back in your own words.
2. **Test, closed-book (6 min).** All four answer the four questions on paper without any help. Compare. This is a tiny classroom test with n of four, so say so. It is not a result; it is a prompt for conversation.
3. **Debrief the difference (4 min).** Who was faster? Who remembers it? Who felt like they learned? What did each pair *skip*?
4. **Design the buddy (10 min).** Together, write the instructions a study buddy should follow. Three lists: **Must do** (ask before telling, check my understanding), **May do** (examples, analogies), **Must never do** (do the assignment for me, hide that it is guessing). Test it with the AI and revise once.
5. **Catch Card (3 min).** What did the AI say that was confident but not in the reading? What would a real teacher have caught?
6. **Driver's Ledger and pitch outline (5 min).** The line that matters: **what part of learning did we decide must stay human, and why?**

## Debrief questions

- When is the answer machine the right tool? (Rarely a flat "never." A calculation you already understand is a good hand-over.)
- What does a teacher or classmate give you that the buddy cannot?
- Whose job is it to decide where help becomes cheating?

## Unplugged fallback

The coach plays the AI twice: once as the answer machine (reads a short summary aloud) and once as the coach (asks questions and gives hints). The test and debrief run as written.

## Watch-outs

- Do not present this as research. It is a four-person classroom demo.
- Do not scold. Students use AI for school. Meet them there.
- Do not tell students what UConn's academic integrity policy allows. Point them to their own instructors.
