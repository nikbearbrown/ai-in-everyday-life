# 10. Pitch and Judging

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Shared by every lab.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** Each group presents a three-minute pitch, then takes one question. Pitches are scored on **two hands**: how well they mastered the tool, and how well they kept the human judgment. **Why it works this way.** If we only scored the idea, we would reward the group that got the shiniest AI output. If we only scored caution, we would reward the group that did nothing. The day teaches both, so the score does too. **What happens.** Four or five pitches, short feedback, and recognition for the group that was the best *driver*, not the best passenger.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 20 to 30 minutes, depending on the format (see [RUN-OF-SHOW](../RUN-OF-SHOW.md)) |
| **Judges** | A graduate student, the faculty or staff anchor, and a UConn host if offered |
| **Materials** | Judging sheets, a timer, small recognitions (stickers or small swag) |

## The pitch (3 minutes plus 1 for a question)

Every group covers the same four beats, in order, using their Driver's Canvas:

1. **The problem, as observed.** One real person, one real friction.
2. **The artifact.** What we built with AI. *Show it* on screen or on paper. No describing it.
3. **The catch.** One thing the AI got wrong, made up or assumed, and how we caught it.
4. **The ledger.** What the AI did, what we checked, what a human signs, and what we did not check.

## The two-hand rubric (score 1 to 4 per line)

**Hand 1. Mastering the tool**
- **Spec:** Did they say exactly what the AI must match, may decide and must ask about?
- **Direct:** Did they iterate, or accept the first draft?
- **Something real:** Is there an artifact we can see?

**Hand 2. Keeping it human**
- **The right problem:** Is it observed, and framed so a different framing would need a different fix?
- **The catch:** A real error, found with a check independent of the AI?
- **The signature:** A named human owner and an honest "what we did not check"?

**Clarity:** Could someone outside the group follow it?

## Recognitions

Give three, so most groups win something:

- **Best Driver** (highest combined score).
- **Best Catch** (the most instructive error found).
- **Best Problem** (the best-observed, best-framed problem).

Add one more if the room needs it: **Best "We Did Not Check."**

## Feedback rules for judges

- Say one thing the group did well in *both* hands.
- Ask one question only a human could answer, such as "Who would call you if this broke?"
- Never score the AI's polish. Score the group's decisions.
- Never ask a student to reveal personal information.

## Unplugged

Pitches work on paper. Students can read off the canvas.

## Watch-outs

- A pitch that says "the AI did everything" and a pitch that says "we did not use AI" are both missing a hand. Say so kindly.
- Keep strict time. A timekeeper holds up cards at 2:00 and 3:00.
