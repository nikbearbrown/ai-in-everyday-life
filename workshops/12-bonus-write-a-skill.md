# 12. Bonus: Write a Skill

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Optional. 30 minutes. Best for the half-day format or the more technical table.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** Students write a **skill**: a short, written procedure that tells an AI how to do one task they care about, including when to stop and ask a human. Then they hand it to another group, who test it. **Why run it.** A skill is the smallest unit of both commitments. Writing one is **mastering the tool** (you must say precisely what you want), and the stop-and-ask lines are **keeping what is human** (you decide where judgment stays with a person). Skills are also how professionals share working methods with AI. **What students leave with.** A skill card that another person could actually follow, and the experience of seeing a stranger's vagueness break a procedure.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 30 minutes |
| **Group** | Pairs, then swap |
| **Materials** | Skill template cards (below), one example printed skill, one shared laptop per group if available |
| **Tech** | Optional. The test can be run by a human reader instead of an AI. |

## The skill template

A skill card has five parts:

1. **Name:** two or three words.
2. **Use when:** the trigger. *When someone asks me to ...*
3. **Steps:** numbered, specific, in order.
4. **Rules:** what it must always or never do.
5. **Stop and ask:** the exact situations where it must hand back to a human, written as testable conditions.

## Run of show

1. **Look at a real one (4 min).** Read one printed skill from the library. Point at each of the five parts.
2. **Pick a task (3 min).** Each pair picks something they do repeatedly: summarizing a reading, planning a study week, writing a polite email, comparing two options.
3. **Write it (10 min).** Fill the five parts. The test of the **Stop and ask** line: *if I handed this to a stranger, could they tell exactly when to stop?*
4. **Swap and test (8 min).** Give your card to another pair. They run it (with an AI, or by reading it aloud as the AI). Where did it break? Where did the reader have to guess?
5. **Fix (3 min).** Revise one line based on what broke.
6. **Debrief (2 min).** Which part was hardest to write? (Usually *Stop and ask.* That is the human part.)

## Say this out loud

"If a stranger cannot follow your procedure, an AI cannot either. Being clear is a skill. Knowing when to stop and ask is a judgment. You just practiced both."

## Facilitator notes

- Do not require installing anything. The test is about the *writing.*
- The strongest cards have a **Stop and ask** that names a real condition ("if the email mentions money or a deadline") rather than a feeling ("if unsure").
- Offer the more technical pairs a stretch: add a "what I did not test" line.

## Unplugged

Entirely paper. A human reader plays the AI.

## Watch-outs

- No private information in a skill card.
- Do not claim a skill "works" because the AI produced something. Check it.
