# 01. Intro: The World Has Changed

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. Whole room, 20 minutes, then a 5-minute break while groups form.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A fast, hands-on opening. A student builds something live with an AI, the room tries to catch the AI being wrong, and each track coach shows a finished example in one minute. **Why run it.** The world has changed: the first draft of almost anything is now free. Students need to see that happen, then see why it is not enough. Two commitments follow: master the tools, and keep what is irreducibly human. **What students leave with.** They pick a track, and they know the three habits they will use all hour: **spec it, catch it, sign it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 20 minutes plus a 5-minute break |
| **Lead** | Master class lead |
| **Materials** | Projector, one laptop with an AI chat, six printed AI answers (the Error Bank), the Driver's Ledger on the wall |

## Run of show

1. **A student builds something live (6 min).** A volunteer drives the keyboard. The room picks a tiny thing to build: *a one-page web tool that picks who buys the coffee, fairly.* The student types a one-sentence request and the AI builds it. Run it. Then ask the room: what did it assume? Re-ask with a **spec** (must match, may decide, must ask me) and compare. Say: "The first draft was free. The better draft cost us thirty seconds of thinking. That thinking is the skill."
2. **Catch the AI (6 min).** Groups of four judge six printed AI answers: right, wrong, or can't tell. Name the exact sentence you doubt and how it could be wrong, *before* anyone looks anything up. Sign your answers. Reveal how each was checked. (See the Error Bank prep below.)
3. **Show, do not tell (4 min).** Each of four coaches holds up one finished example from their dry-run, in one minute: an agent answering emails, the Claude Jumpman platformer, a cartoon spaceship variant, an Oregon Trail trip. Real work, run live or shown from a saved recording.
4. **The two hands (4 min).** Put the Driver's Ledger on the wall. Say: "You will do two things. Get good at driving the tool. And protect the parts only you can do: choosing the right problem, smelling a wrong answer, being with the people you build for, deciding together, and signing your name. Every group ends with a ledger: what the AI did, what we checked, what a human signs, and what we did not check."
5. **Pick a track (during the break).** Four tracks, five students each. Students choose. If a track is oversubscribed, the lead balances by coding experience.

## Prep: the Error Bank

Before the day, the lead builds six answer cards from **real** AI chats they ran. Each card shows the question and the AI's answer, and the lead has verified the truth from an independent source and kept the answer key. Two wrong, two right, two half-right. Good subtle errors: an invented citation, a confident number with no source, a unit mix-up, a summary that adds a claim the original did not make. Use real ones. If you are unsure of the truth of a card, drop it.

## Say this out loud

"Fluent is not the same as true. The first draft is free now. What you do next is what makes you worth hiring, worth trusting, and worth listening to."

## Unplugged fallback

If the Wi-Fi fails, show the saved transcript of the live build from the lead's practice run and say so: "This ran earlier today." The catch round is paper.

## Watch-outs

- A student drives, not the lead.
- Never quote statistics about jobs, speed or adoption. Say only what the room just saw.
- If the live build fails, stop and say so. That is the lesson.
