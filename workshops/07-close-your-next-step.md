# 07. Close: Your Next Step

*Status: DRAFT. Program facts need confirmation from SEIS admissions before use.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A 10-minute close in which graduate students and a faculty or staff anchor connect the day to graduate study in information systems. **Why it matters.** Students just built something with an AI and kept their own judgment. A degree in information systems builds both, in a place where students design the system around the AI: framing the problem, specifying it, checking it and signing it. The graduate students running the tracks are the strongest evidence. **What students leave with.** An honest sense of what MSIS and MSIS-Bridge are, who they are for, and how the people who coached today got there.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 10 minutes (25 in the half-day, with Q&A) |
| **Lead** | Faculty or staff anchor plus the graduate students |
| **Materials** | Take-home card, flyer, swag, program facts **supplied by SEIS admissions** |

## What to say (and what not to make up)

**Do say** (and confirm before using):
- What MSIS and MSIS-Bridge are, in one sentence each, from SEIS's own materials.
- Who MSIS-Bridge is for: students from non-technical backgrounds. Published spotlight stories are real examples.
- Where graduates have gone, using only **published** spotlight stories.

**Do not say** until admissions confirms in writing: tuition, credit counts, deadlines, scholarships, admission rates, placement rates, class sizes or course titles. Mark any such claim `[CONFIRM WITH SEIS]`.

## Run of show (10 minutes)

1. **Callback (2 min).** "Today you did two things: you got your hands on the tools, and you kept the judgment. That is the job."
2. **The graduate students (4 min).** Each coach says one sentence: where I came from, what I did before, one thing I did with AI that I would not have known to check. First names only.
3. **The programs (3 min).** The anchor describes MSIS and MSIS-Bridge using confirmed facts.
4. **Next step (1 min).** Hand out the take-home card with a contact, and a link to this repository.

## Half-day version: graduate student Q&A (add 15 minutes)

Seat three coaches at the front. Ask: What do you wish you knew as an undergraduate? What surprised you about using AI at work? What do you still do yourself on purpose?

## Say this out loud

"The tools will keep changing. What lasts is knowing what to ask, how to check it, and being willing to put your name on it."

## Watch-outs

- No pressure to apply. This is information.
- No claims about hiring, salary or placement.
- Advising or transfer questions go to the anchor and the official contact.
