# 11. Your Next Step: SEIS, MSIS and MSIS-Bridge

*Status: DRAFT. Program facts need confirmation from SEIS admissions before use.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A 10 to 25 minute close in which graduate students and a faculty or staff anchor connect the day's work to graduate study at SEIS. **Why it matters.** The day told students two things: master the tools, and keep what is human. A degree in information systems builds both, in a place where students design systems and sign their work. The graduate students running the day are the strongest evidence of that. **What students leave with.** An honest sense of what MSIS and MSIS-Bridge are, who they are for, and how the people who taught today got there, plus the take-home card and a contact.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 10 minutes (90 and 120 min formats), 25 minutes (half-day, with Q&A) |
| **Lead** | Faculty or staff anchor plus the graduate students |
| **Materials** | Take-home card, flyer, swag, a short list of program facts **supplied by SEIS admissions** |

## What to say (and what to not make up)

**Do say** (and confirm with SEIS before using):
- What MSIS and MSIS-Bridge are, in one sentence each, from SEIS's own materials.
- Who MSIS-Bridge is for: students coming from a non-technical background. The spotlight stories are real examples. One is a career change from paralegal to data analyst through the Bridge program.
- Where graduates have gone, using only **published** SEIS spotlight stories (they are already public on the college site), such as co-op roles in healthcare technology and at large technology firms.

**Do not say** until SEIS admissions confirms in writing: tuition, credit counts, deadlines, scholarships, admission rates, co-op placement rates, class sizes, or specific course titles. Put `[CONFIRM WITH SEIS]` on any slide that carries one.

## Run of show (10-minute version)

1. **Callback (2 min).** "Today you did two things: you got your hands on the tools, and you kept the judgment. That is the job."
2. **The graduate students (4 min).** Each coach says one sentence: *where I came from, what I did before SEIS, one thing I did with AI that I would not have known to check.* Real, short, honest. First names only.
3. **The programs (3 min).** The anchor describes MSIS and MSIS-Bridge using confirmed facts. Name that SEIS teaches students to design the system around the AI: framing the problem, specifying it, checking it and signing it.
4. **Next step (1 min).** Hand out the take-home card with the contact. Offer a QR to the public repo.

## Half-day version: graduate student Q&A (add 15 min)

Seat three coaches at the front. Ask them: *What do you wish you knew as an undergraduate? What surprised you about using AI at work? What do you still do yourself on purpose?* Take student questions. Do not let a coach speak for the admissions process.

## Say this out loud

"The tools will keep changing. What lasts is knowing what to ask, how to check it, and being willing to put your name on it."

## Watch-outs

- No pressure to apply. This is information.
- No claims about hiring, salary or placement from a slide or from memory.
- If a UConn student asks about transferring credit or an advising matter, point them to the anchor and the official admissions contact.
