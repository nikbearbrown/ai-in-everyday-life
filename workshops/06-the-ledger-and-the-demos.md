# 06. The Driver's Ledger and the Demos

*Status: DRAFT, not yet dry-run. The one-page ledger is filled in during the build. The demos are 20 minutes.*

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A one-page record every group fills in while they build, and a demo round where each group runs what they made live and answers for it. **Why it exists.** It is where the two commitments meet on paper. A group cannot finish without writing down what the AI did, how they checked it, who signs, and what they did not check. And the demo is scored on both hands: did you make something real, and did you keep your own judgment. **What students leave with.** A working thing, and a one-page record of their own work that they can show an employer or a professor.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Ledger: throughout the build. Demos: 20 minutes (4 groups, 4 minutes each, 4 minutes buffer) |
| **Judges** | A graduate student, the faculty or staff anchor, and a host from the visiting group if offered |

## The Driver's Ledger (print one per group)

**Group: ________  Track: ________**

**1. What we built.** One line.

**2. The spec.**
| Must match | May decide | Must stop and ask a human |
|---|---|---|

**3. The Catch Card.** What the AI did or said: ________ What was true or right: ________ How we checked, with something that fails differently than the AI (a person, a plan, a playtest, a source): ________

**4. The ledger.**
| What the AI did | What we checked, and how | What a human decided and signs | Why that part stays human |
|---|---|---|---|

**5. What we did not check.** One honest line. Required.

## The demo (4 minutes per group)

1. **The catch (1 min). Start here, before anything is shown.** "What did you catch?" One thing the AI got wrong or made up, how you caught it, and whether it matched what you predicted.
2. **What you made (2 min).** Now run it live. Not a description. The thing running.
3. **The ledger (1 min).** What the AI did, what you checked, who signs, and what you did not check.

**Why the catch comes first.** A demo that opens on the finished build rewards the prettiest output, and the AI made the output. Starting with the catch puts the group's own judgment first.

If the live run fails, say so and show the saved recording. That counts as honesty, not as failure.

## The two-hand rubric (score 1 to 4 per line)

**Hand 1. Mastering the tool**
- **Spec:** Did they say what the AI must match, may decide and must ask about?
- **Direct:** Did they iterate, or accept the first draft?
- **Something real:** Does it run?

**Hand 2. Keeping it human**
- **The catch:** A real error, found with a check independent of the AI?
- **The gate:** A named human owner and a clear place where a person decides?
- **The honest line:** A real "what we did not check"?

## Recognitions

Three, so most groups win something:

- **Best Driver** (highest combined score).
- **Best Catch** (the most instructive error found).
- **Best "We Did Not Check"** (the most honest line).

## Judge feedback rules

- Say one thing the group did well in *both* hands.
- Ask one question only a human could answer, such as "Who would you call if this broke?"
- Never score the AI's polish. Score the group's decisions. Ask "what did you catch?" before "what did you make?" in the questions too.
- Never ask a student for personal information.

## Unplugged

The ledger is paper. A demo can be a paper walk-through if the screen fails.

## Watch-outs

- "The AI did everything" and "we did not use AI" each miss a hand. Say so kindly.
- Strict time: hold up cards at 3:00 and 4:00.
- A ledger row that says "looks good" or "AI checked it" does not count. Ask: *looked at what, against what?*
