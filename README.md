# AI in Everyday Life: workshop materials

## Executive summary

**What this is.** The complete materials for a hands-on workshop called **AI in Everyday Life: Solving Real-World Problems Through Innovation**. It is a workshop, and this repository is where the materials live so anyone can download and run it. It was designed for about 20 undergraduates from mixed technical and non-technical backgrounds, taught by graduate students, with no coding required. It was first prepared for a visit to the Northeastern University Information Systems department (SEIS) on November 6, 2026.

**Why use it.** The world has changed. AI now produces a first draft of almost anything in seconds, and students are graduating into that. So the workshop teaches two things together and refuses to teach one without the other:

1. **Master the tools.** Spec it, direct it, check it, chain it. Students keep their hands on the keyboard.
2. **Keep what is irreducibly human.** Notice the right problem, smell a wrong answer, be with people, decide together, own the consequence.

**What you get.** 13 facilitator-ready workshops (an icebreaker, three whole-room demos, five small-group labs, a worksheet, a pitch round, a close, and a bonus), a run of show that fits a 90-minute, 2-hour or half-day slot, and a guide for the people who teach it. Every module has an unplugged fallback in case the Wi-Fi fails.

**Status.** These are drafts. No workshop has been run with a live audience yet. Expect to adapt them, and please tell us what breaks.

## How to use this

1. Read [THE-THROUGH-LINE.md](THE-THROUGH-LINE.md). It is the idea behind everything.
2. Pick a format in [RUN-OF-SHOW.md](RUN-OF-SHOW.md).
3. Give each facilitator [FACILITATOR-GUIDE.md](FACILITATOR-GUIDE.md) and their workshop guide.
4. Run each guide once with people who have not seen it before the real session.

**To download:** use the green **Code** button, then **Download ZIP**, or clone the repository.

## Notes

- The ideas are teaching lenses from the author's courses and writing. They are not research findings, and the materials quote no statistics.
- Any program facts in the close (workshop 11) must be confirmed with your own program office. The repo invents none.
- Facilitators appear by first name and last initial only. No students are ever named.
